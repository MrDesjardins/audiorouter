01 / Understand

The idea, before the settings.

Two separate shelves shape the low and high ends. Bass and Treble each have their own gain and transition frequency; this is not one movable peaking band.

Source→Bass & Treble→Meter → Output

Use it in AudioRouter

  1. Begin with Bass and Treble at 0 dB so the shelves are flat.
  2. For boom, try Bass −3 dB and move Bass frequency lower to focus on the bottom rather than all low mids.
  3. For brightness, try Treble +2 dB. Raise Treble frequency to affect mainly the upper end.
  4. Match output loudness before comparing Bypass; check headroom after boosts.

What success looks like

Shelves change broad regions. Frequency moves the transition; it is not a separate gain adjustment.

If it does not work

If voice sounds thin or harsh, reduce the amount or move the transition away from important voice frequencies.

Select the node to open Properties. Click its name to rename it. While playing, supported parameter-only edits save after a short pause. For structural edits, use Save when the canvas marks a change. Enabled and Bypass are distinct live controls; see the in-app help for their behavior.

04 / Put it to work

Start with a useful example.

Example 1

Reduce a boomy microphone

  1. Try Bass −3 dB at 500 Hz.
  2. Keep Treble at 0 dB while evaluating.
  3. Compare bypass at a matched listening level.
Example 2

Focus on the frequency extremes

  1. Set Bass frequency to 120 Hz and Treble frequency to 6000 Hz.
  2. Keep your chosen gain amounts and compare the narrower regions affected.

These are starting points. Compare at a comfortable monitoring level and adjust for your own source.

05 / Get unstuck

Small details that matter.

  • Large boosts can consume headroom.
  • Changing a frequency can make the same gain setting sound much stronger.
